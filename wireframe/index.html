Accounting Books: wireframes for review

Thirteen screens, in the order he would use them. Each screen has five layout options and a summary. They are black-and-white sketches of layout only: colours and final wording come later. Every name, amount and email is invented.

  1. App shell and Home

    Recommended: Option 2, Next Steps.

    He does his books every few weeks, so Home should say what is waiting and give one button per job.

  2. Bank page and importing a Westpac file

    Recommended: Option 5, Plain Summary.

    The preview says what will happen in sentences, so duplicates and bad lines are understood without reading a table.

  3. To review (giving bank lines an account)

    Recommended: Option 1, Review Table.

    The fastest way through 50 lines. Updated from his QuickBooks screens: Spent and Received, From/To, Match or account, and Post.

  4. Bank rules

    Recommended: Option 1, List and Edit Panel.

    Works like the QuickBooks rules he uses now, including rules that post by themselves, plus "Do this every time?" after posting.

  5. Transactions and chart of accounts

    Recommended: Option 1, Tables.

    Like his QuickBooks expense list and chart of accounts, with each entry's history one click away.

  6. Invoices from the Admin Dashboard

    Recommended: Option 2, Linked Folder.

    He keeps making invoices and quotes in his Admin Dashboard. This app receives the issued invoices, shows who owes what, and matches payments.

  7. Customers and suppliers (contacts)

    Recommended: Option 1, List and Contact Page, with saving as you go.

    Names are saved the first time they appear, so he never types them twice, and clicking a customer shows all their invoices and payments.

  8. Reports and the BAS worksheet

    Recommended: Option 3, Quarter Close.

    A short checklist makes sure the quarter is only locked after the lines are reviewed and the accounts reconciled.

  9. Customising a report (columns and rows)

    Recommended: Option 2, Side Panel.

    He ticks the columns and rows he wants and sees the report change at once, then saves it as his own report.

  10. Sharing with the accountant

    Recommended: Option 2, People First, with Option 4's "What accepting does" box.

    Shows in one place what the accountant can see, with one button to stop it, and what each change would do before it is accepted.

  11. Working with the Google Sheet (free-form, formulas)

    Recommended: all five together, built around Option 3.

    These are parts that fit together, not rivals: preview before publishing, the real sheet inside the app or beside it in Chrome, ready formulas, and his own tabs kept.

  12. Settings

    Recommended: Option 3, Status Cards.

    A disconnected Google account or stopped backups show at the top, where they cannot be missed.

  13. Help and guides

    Recommended: Option 1, Like Admin Dashboard.

    The same Help he already knows from his Admin Dashboard, with a help button on each page.

  14. Invoices and bills

    Not drawn yet. Only needed if he uses invoices or bills in QuickBooks today (client question 1).

How to use these

Open a screen, then click the numbered tabs to compare the options. Inside each window, the "Show" buttons switch between situations, such as a brand-new book or a file with a problem. Buttons with a "…" open the box they would open in the app. The numbered circles match the notes under each window.

Write down his choices and changes in context/wireframe-feedback.md.
